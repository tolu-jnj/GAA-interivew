Opening

Tell us about yourself and why this role?

I am a Staff Engineer in CAR-T Business Excellence, and my work sits at the intersection of regulated operations, analytics, automation, and transformation. In LVV, I have built and governed Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 performance-management solutions, aligned metrics across COMET, TrackWise, SUMMIT, and CURVE, and helped move leadership reviews from ad hoc reporting toward standard definitions, recurring decision cadences, and scalable dashboards. I also led analytics around nonconformances and investigation quality, including a 2025 baseline and Q1 year-over-year analysis across 354 records, plus an AI-enabled NC/CAPA draft-review approach grounded in approved procedures. What attracts me to this role is the opportunity to apply that same combination of technical building, control discipline, and adoption leadership at enterprise scale. I would bring the perspective of someone who understands both the data product and the operating process it is meant to improve.

Motivation

Why move from CAR-T manufacturing to Corporate Global Audit & Assurance?

I see this as a scale shift, not a departure from my core work. In CAR-T I learned how to turn highly regulated, fragmented operational data into trusted decisions. GAA would let me apply that capability across functions, geographies, and technology domains. The business problem is also familiar: define the decision, establish trustworthy data, standardize the workflow, automate repeatable work, and keep human judgment where risk is high. The leadership program is especially attractive because I want to grow from leading cross-functional transformation into formally leading a digital analytics team.

Motivation

Why should we select you over a traditional audit or finance candidate?

A traditional audit candidate may bring deeper audit methodology on day one. My differentiator is that I can partner with auditors to build the digital capabilities that change how audits are performed. I understand controls, evidence, traceability, change control, and risk from GMP operations, while also bringing hands-on experience with Power BI, Databricks, Power Platform, data pipelines, APIs, and application development. I would not pretend to replace audit judgment. I would help turn that judgment into governed, repeatable, scalable analytics and automation.

Role fit

What do you understand this job to be?

The role owns the end-to-end design and implementation of analytics, RPA, AI, OCR, and other tools used in internal audits or investigations. The outcome is not simply a dashboard. It is improved process effectiveness and efficiency, new insight, standardization, repeatability, scalability, and a better end-user experience. It also includes governance for data quality and change control, code review and testing, global training, influencing GAA leadership, and potentially leading one to three people.

3E

How would you apply 3E Value Realization to a digital audit solution?

I would define value before building. For Efficiency, I would baseline hours, cycle time, repetitive extraction effort, and rework, then measure the same result at lower cost. For Effectiveness, I would measure improved outcomes without increased cost, such as broader population coverage, earlier risk detection, fewer false negatives, or better control-test consistency. For Experience, I would measure stakeholder satisfaction, usability, time-to-insight, and adoption. I would align the KPI, data source, classification, and target with Finance, then compare realized value with the baseline after go-live. For an audit-risk application, I would resist claiming value from activity counts alone. The value should connect to a decision or risk outcome.

Data theory

Explain Data Operating Theory and how you use it.

I use it as a way to think about data before requesting a deliverable. I start with the business decision and governance, then separate the lifecycle into six activities: Governance, Generation, Collection and Standardization, Aggregation, Analysis, and Application. That separation prevents a common failure mode where ingestion, cleansing, model logic, and user interaction are mixed into one opaque solution. In my LVV work, I applied this thinking by documenting metric owners, source systems, refresh cadence, definitions, and validation checks before scaling Tier dashboards, and by distinguishing the NC analytical logic from the application that users interact with.

Data theory

How would you structure an audit analytics repository?

I would make the repository reflect the data lifecycle: 1-Governance, 2-Generation, 3-Collection_Standardization, 4-Aggregation, 5-Analysis, and 6-Application. Governance would point to business ownership, technical ownership, access instructions, documentation, versions, privacy and control requirements. Generation would contain source read and write logic plus identifiers that verify expected inputs. Collection and Standardization would harmonize schemas, units, names, datatypes, and keys. Aggregation would create derived features supporting the analysis. Analysis would contain the actual risk or audit model. Application would contain orchestration, APIs, user interaction, tests, operational health, monitoring, and feedback.

Technical

Walk us through an end-to-end data product you built.

A strong example is LVV Tier performance management. The initial problem was fragmented manual reporting across Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3. I mapped the workflows, identified the decisions and escalation paths, documented metric definitions, owners, refresh cadence, calculation methods, and source alignment across COMET, TrackWise, SUMMIT, and CURVE. I deployed Tier 1 and Tier 2 digital boards with 7- and 30-day rollups, action aging, open escalations, and leadership-ready views. I also defined a 48-hour action rule and trained facilitators. During a 20-workday review window, the overall MET rate was 81.4% across 215 entries, with two open escalations. The key lesson was that the dashboard was only one layer. The value came from the operating model, definitions, accountability, and sustainment.

Technical

Describe your Power BI experience.

I use Power BI in the context of governed operational reporting, not only visualization. My approach is to begin with the decision and grain, define measures and ownership, reconcile outputs to source data, and design separate views for operator action and leadership oversight. In LVV, my dashboard work has covered NCs, action aging, schedule attainment, Tier performance, and review-and-release cycle-time visibility. I would bring the same discipline to audit analytics by separating reusable semantic measures from presentation logic and by documenting refresh, lineage, and validation.

Technical

What is your Tableau experience?

My strongest production experience is in Power BI rather than Tableau. I would be transparent about that. The transferable skills are data modeling, calculation logic, filtering, performance-aware visualization, UX, governance, and stakeholder adoption. I can ramp quickly on Tableau, but I would not overstate direct depth. My value on day one would be in framing the decision, building trusted data products, and applying visualization principles consistently across platforms.

Technical

How strong are you in Python?

I use Python as part of my analytics and automation toolkit, particularly for data preparation, repeatable analysis, and logic that is better handled outside a spreadsheet. My engineering discipline is to keep ingestion, standardization, aggregation, analysis, and application concerns explicit, test assumptions, and document inputs and outputs. I would describe myself as applied and growing rather than claim to be a specialist software engineer. Where the work is production-critical, I expect code review, tests, version control, logging, and clear operational ownership.

Technical

Tell us about APIs and automation.

I have worked with automation and API-oriented solutions through Power Platform, application development, and digital workflows. My design rule is to use deterministic automation when inputs, rules, and outcomes are stable, and reserve AI or agents for tasks requiring interpretation or dynamic reasoning. For an audit use case, I would define the contract, authentication, expected schema, error handling, retries, logging, and ownership before connecting systems. I would also separate read, transform, decision, and write-back steps so the control points remain auditable.

Technical

How would you ingest SAP and BRAVO data for a risk-scoring application?

I would first define the audit-planning decision at the correct grain, for example business unit, process, or legal entity. In Generation, I would identify approved SAP and BRAVO sources, extraction method, access owner, cadence, and expected schema. In Collection and Standardization, I would harmonize entity keys, time periods, currencies, risk taxonomies, and missing-value rules. In Aggregation, I would derive transparent features such as transaction volume, control exceptions, prior findings, change velocity, or data-quality indicators. In Analysis, I would begin with an explainable weighted score before considering a predictive model. In Application, I would show the score, drivers, provenance, confidence or data-quality caveats, and allow authorized human review. I would never let the score become an unexplained audit conclusion.

Technical

How would you combine qualitative and quantitative data into a risk score?

I would create a common unit of analysis and keep the source type visible. Quantitative measures would be normalized against appropriate peer groups or baselines. Qualitative ratings would use anchored definitions so a rating of three means the same thing across assessors. I would document weighting, directionality, missing-data treatment, and override rules. I would back-test the score against historical audit outcomes, perform sensitivity analysis, and monitor drift. The interface should show both the score and its drivers so an auditor can challenge it. A human remains accountable for the annual audit-plan decision.

Technical

What tests would you require before releasing an audit analytic?

I would test each lifecycle layer. For Generation: credentials, source availability, read/write access, file type, schema, and empty inputs. For Collection and Standardization: duplicate rules, required columns, datatypes, valid keys, naming, units, and conformance. For Aggregation: engineered-feature sanity, missing values, outliers, and reproducibility. For Analysis: training-target consistency, leakage, performance, bias where relevant, drift, and explainability. For Application: dead buttons, downloads, filters, permissions, multi-user behavior, graceful failure, logging, performance, and browser behavior. I would also run regression tests against a golden output and define monitoring and rollback before go-live.

AI

Where would you use AI, ML, OCR, or RPA in audit?

I would match the technology to the work. OCR is useful for extracting structured fields from invoices, contracts, or evidence packages. Deterministic RPA fits repeatable retrieval or reconciliation steps with stable rules. ML can prioritize anomalies or estimate risk where a validated historical signal exists. Generative AI can assist with evidence synthesis, issue-taxonomy mapping, draft workpapers, or retrieval across approved knowledge. I would keep audit conclusions, materiality judgments, allegations, and irreversible actions behind explicit human review. My LVV NC/CAPA draft-review pilot followed that pattern: the model supported a rubric grounded in approved procedures, while accountable individuals retained the decision.

AI

How would you govern an AI-assisted investigation tool?

I would define the approved use case, prohibited uses, data classification, permitted sources, access control, retention, audit logging, validation criteria, human reviewer, and exception path. Outputs should cite source evidence and distinguish fact from hypothesis. The solution should not autonomously make a compliance conclusion or write back to a system of record without approval. I would test known failure modes such as missing context, contradictory evidence, fabricated citations, prompt injection, and sensitive-data exposure, then monitor both technical performance and business impact.

Audit

You are not an auditor. How will you succeed?

I would treat audit methodology as a domain I must learn quickly and respectfully. I already work in a regulated environment where evidence, procedures, traceability, segregation of duties, change control, CAPA, and defensibility matter. I would partner with audit SMEs to define the control objective and acceptance criteria, while I own the digital design, data lineage, testing, and adoption. My role is not to turn technology into audit judgment; it is to make high-quality audit judgment more scalable and better informed.

Audit

What is your understanding of SOX and continuous monitoring?

At a practical level, SOX requires reliable internal control over financial reporting and evidence that key controls are designed and operating effectively. Continuous monitoring uses recurring analytics to surface exceptions or changing risk instead of waiting for periodic manual review. For a digital analyst, the obligation is to preserve source lineage, rule versioning, completeness, access controls, reproducibility, and evidence. I would confirm specific GAA policy and methodology with audit leadership rather than assume that manufacturing controls map one-for-one to financial controls.

Leadership

Tell us about influencing without authority.

When I developed LVV Tier 1 and Tier 2 daily management, I did not own Operations, Warehouse, Planning, Quality, or EHS. I created shared charters, clarified decision rights and escalation paths, defined the 48-hour action rule, trained facilitators, and built visible dashboards that made the process useful to each function. Adoption came from embedding the solution in the daily work and rotating facilitation, not from asking people to view another report. The process continued during my paternity leave, which was an important sustainment test.

Leadership

Tell us about leading global or cross-site work.

For the Advanced Therapies Lentivirus scorecard, I served as the business process owner and collaborated with Raritan and Sassenheim stakeholders to consolidate requirements for the OPM platform. We aligned KPI categories, time views, metric definitions, and phased tool requirements. My role was to translate local operational needs into a standardized design without erasing valid site differences. That experience is directly relevant to GAA, where scalable solutions must work across regions and business contexts.

Leadership

How do you coach or give constructive feedback?

I set the standard, show the evidence, explain the impact, and agree on the next behavior. In digital work, feedback should be specific enough to act on, whether it is a metric-definition gap, an untested edge case, or unclear ownership after go-live. I also separate capability gaps from commitment gaps. My Lean facilitation and Tier facilitator training taught me that sustainable coaching includes practice, observation, and follow-up, not a one-time presentation.

Leadership

How would you lead 1–3 direct reports?

I would give each person clear outcomes, decision rights, quality standards, and development goals. Work would be visible through a prioritized backlog tied to GAA outcomes. I would review products at decision points rather than micromanage every task, and I would require documentation, peer review, tests, and stakeholder feedback as part of done. I would use regular one-on-ones for growth and remove blockers, while making accountability explicit. The goal would be a team that can operate reliably without becoming dependent on me.

Behavioral

Tell us about a project that did not go as planned.

A recurring lesson in my dashboard work is that automating a poorly defined metric creates faster disagreement, not better decisions. When definitions, ownership, or grain were not aligned, I stepped back from the tool, documented the business definition and calculation, reconciled source data, and brought the stakeholders together before continuing. The lesson I now carry is to treat governance as the first activity, not the final documentation step.

Behavioral

How do you prioritize competing requests?

I first clarify the decision, customer, risk, urgency, and 3E value. Then I separate critical control or compliance needs from convenience requests and score the remainder on value, feasibility, data readiness, adoption impact, and time to learning. I make trade-offs visible to sponsors and maintain a transparent backlog. I prefer a small governed pilot that proves the decision value over a large tool with unclear ownership.

Scenario

Your risk model conflicts with the audit team's judgment. What do you do?

I would not treat disagreement as model failure or human error by default. I would review the data grain, source completeness, feature definitions, weighting, time period, and any known context not represented in the model. I would show the drivers and run a sensitivity check. If the auditor has legitimate contextual evidence, the process should allow a documented override. The disagreement itself becomes feedback for model improvement. Final audit-planning accountability stays with the authorized leaders.

Scenario

A senior leader asks you to skip testing to meet a deadline. What do you do?

I would explain the specific risk and offer a controlled alternative: reduce scope, release to a limited pilot, use read-only outputs, label limitations, add manual verification, or delay the high-risk feature. I would not silently remove required controls. In audit and investigation work, an unreliable output can undermine both the decision and trust in the broader digital program.

Closing

What would your first 90 days look like?

First, learn the GAA audit lifecycle, stakeholders, systems, control expectations, and existing product portfolio, including ARA. Second, inventory current requests and products across the six data activities, documenting owners, users, sources, refresh, tests, operational health, and value measures. Third, select one use case with a clear decision, accessible data, manageable risk, and visible 3E value. Deliver a governed pilot with acceptance criteria, Finance-aligned value measures, user training, monitoring, and a scale or stop decision. In parallel, I would build relationships with auditors, Finance, Compliance, and technology partners so the solution fits the operating model rather than sitting beside it.
